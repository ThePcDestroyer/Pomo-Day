The idea

Physics, not animation.

Nothing in here is a canned effect. Every node has a density, a strength, a melting point and a heat capacity, and what you see on screen is simply what those numbers do when something fast arrives.

Tilt the same plate and the bullet can skip off it. Make it thinner and a plug punches out of the back. Neither one is triggered by a script.

How it works

Nodes, bonds and heat.

Nodes and bonds

Every object is a lattice of nodes joined by bonds. A bond stretches like a spring, flows like metal when pushed too far, and snaps when stretched past its limit.

Strength that changes

Johnson-Cook plasticity makes metal harder when it is worked, stronger when loaded fast and weaker when hot. Bending and friction turn into heat, and heat makes it softer.

Cracks and fragments

Ceramics break with very little stretch and crush into pieces, while steel tears only after a lot of deformation. Loose pieces collide and rub, so spall, plugs and crumbling bullets just happen.

FAQ

Good to know.

Is it accurate?

It is a simplified 2D slice with rough engineering values. It is good for seeing how materials, thickness and angles compare, but it is not test data.

Can I use it to design armor?

No. It is for learning and curiosity only and must not be used to design, rate or certify real protective equipment.

Does it cost anything or collect my data?

It is free and has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. Saved scenes stay in your own browser. See the privacy page.

Does it work on a tablet?

Yes. Use two fingers to pinch-zoom and pan, and one finger to move bullets and armor. Tablets start with coarse node spacing so it runs smoothly.

Why is it slow on my device?

The nodes are simulated live on your own processor. Pick a coarser node spacing on phones and older laptops.
